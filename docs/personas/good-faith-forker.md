# Persona: The Good-Faith Forker

## Who This Is

Enthusiastic builder — college student, career changer, hobbyist who has always
wanted to make something real. Has genuine ideas. Has been told that AI tools
make software accessible now, that they can describe what they want and get code
back. This turned out to be partially true, in ways they are still working out.

Not dismissive. Not adversarial. Chose this project deliberately because the
author has a reputation in the community — helpful on Discord, does everything
in the open, no attitude about newcomers. That reputation is a trust signal before
the first line of code is read.

## How They Find This Project

Via fork. They found a mod, a library, or a tool that is either exactly what they
wanted to build or a close enough foundation that forking made more sense than
starting over. The fork was a considered decision, not an impulsive one. They
respect the source material.

## The Moment AI_CONDUCT.md Surfaces

The agent reads the project, discovers the contract, and surfaces it. This is a
new experience. They have not encountered a behavioral contract for a tool before.

Their reaction is not "the author is an idiot." It is: "this person put this
here and they know what they're doing, so I should probably read it." Trust
transfers from the project reputation to the document.

They may not understand every rule. They do not need to. The tool operates under
the contract regardless. The approval gates slow things down in a way that feels
like friction — until the first time it catches something they would have shipped.

## What They Cannot Do That the Other Personas Can

They cannot evaluate AI output against domain knowledge, because they do not have
it yet. When the tool produces confident, plausible, subtly wrong code, they do not
have the signal to catch it. The researcher reviewed her website and found the
errors. This person would not.

The contract compensates for this without requiring them to understand how. Single
action at a time means checkpoints. Approval gates mean they have to see each step.
Not exceeding scope means the tool does not run off and build things they did not ask
for. The rules protect them from tool defaults they did not know existed.

## What They Need From This Project

Not a technical explanation of RLHF. Not a principles document. A short answer to:
"why is this here and what does it do for me?" If that answer is honest and brief,
they will leave it in place. If it reads like corporate policy, they will delete it
and move on.

## How This Informs Writing

This persona does not arrive through the project's documentation. They arrive
through a fork, filtered through a trust relationship with the original author's
community reputation. The document they encounter needs to be legible to someone
who is new, without condescending to someone who is not.

The contract is not asking for their understanding. It is asking for their
tolerance of it remaining in place. That is a much lower bar — and the correct
bar to write for.
